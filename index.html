The phenomenon of cyberbullying

Cyberbullying refers to the act of repeatedly harming, threatening, humiliating, or excluding others through online platforms such as games and social media apps. It is a significant ethical issue in the modern digital landscape; while the internet grants people the freedom to express their views, this very freedom also creates the conditions that enable cyberbullying. Such behavior can occur on public platforms or in private chats, and because online content is easily disseminated and preserved, victims may suffer long-lasting effects. Addressing cyberbullying should not be left solely to the victims; instead, responsibility must be shared among individuals, schools, online platforms, and society as a whole.

Shawn: Platform Handling of Online Bullying

Platforms should improve their reporting and content moderation mechanisms. They must ensure that the process for reporting cyberbullying is simple and accessible. Upon receiving a report, the platform is required to promptly review the harmful content—particularly in cases involving threats or persistent harassment—and take decisive action to curb such violations, such as banning accounts or removing the offending remarks.

Shawn: How schools can help address cyberbullying

Schools should implement educational initiatives to help students understand that online behavior can have real-world impacts on others' mental health and reputations. Schools can teach students how to communicate respectfully, recognize cyberbullying, and respond safely. The goal is to prevent cyberbullying before it escalates, rather than simply addressing it after the fact.

Shawn:The government has introduced a real-name registration policy for internet access.

Governments can implement policies requiring real-name verification for social media accounts and prohibiting unverified accounts from posting content, thereby encouraging individuals to take responsibility for their actions. People should think carefully before posting, commenting on, or sharing content involving others. Real-name verification prompts individuals to consider the impact and consequences of their words before speaking online, discouraging them from recklessly harming others; this plays a significant role in curbing cyberbullying.

How are victims of cyberbullying are impacted by its ethical problems

Tristan: Mental health issues

Perpetrators often hide behind fake profiles or untraceable accounts. This removes moral responsibility and leaves victims unable to confront or hold their attackers accountable. This also leaves them with frequent experiences of high levels of anxiety, clinical depression, chronic/stress, and low self-esteem.

Tristan: Physical symptoms

Cyberbullying frequently involves publishing private address and contact details or hacking into accounts to steal and leak private conversations. This can lead to the victims stressing out about all their personal data leaking out. That stress can physically manifests into insomnia, sleep disturbances, headaches, stomach aches, and gastrointestinal issues.

Tristan: Inability to escape

Unlike traditional bullying, digital harassment happens 24/7 everyday and follows victims into their private homes through phones and computers. Online platforms and passive observers often fail in their ethical duty of care, only prioritizing user engagement metrics or remaining silent instead of moderating abuse or supporting victims. This is often led to suicidal ideas and self-harm attempts by victims.
